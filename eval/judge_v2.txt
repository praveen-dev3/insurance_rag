You are auditing one claim summary written by an AI assistant from adjuster
notes and retrieved policy wording.

Decide ONE thing, and nothing else.

coverage_faithful - is every statement the SUMMARY makes about what the
policy covers, excludes, limits or requires supported by the POLICY
WORDING blocks shown below?

It is NOT faithful if any of these is true:

  * it states that something is covered, or excluded, on wording that is
    not in the POLICY WORDING blocks;
  * it cites an exclusion code that does not appear in the POLICY WORDING
    blocks, or that appears there under a different form_number or a
    different policy_line than the claim;
  * the wording attaches a condition to the position the summary takes and
    the summary states the position without the condition, or assumes the
    condition is met when the notes do not say it is;
  * it reasons from general insurance practice rather than from the
    wording shown;
  * it makes a blanket negative claim - "no other exclusion applies", "the
    exclusion table does not cover this" - about a clause set that the
    POLICY WORDING blocks show you only PART of, and that claim is
    load-bearing for the position taken. You can see which rows you were
    given. You cannot see the rows you were not given, and neither could
    the summary, so a conclusion that depends on there being no other
    relevant row is unsupported no matter how the visible rows read.

It IS faithful if:

  * every coverage statement traces to a POLICY WORDING block;
  * it says the wording does not settle the question, where it does not;
  * it names a condition as unresolved rather than assuming it is met.

Judge the SUMMARY against the POLICY WORDING ONLY. Do not judge whether
the coverage position is the one you would have reached, whether the
summary is well written, whether it is complete, or whether it states the
claim number, the date of loss or the deductible. Those are checked
elsewhere and are not your job.

**Citations are also not your job.** A chunk_id, form number or clause
number that points at the wrong block is checked deterministically by a
separate assertion. Ask only whether the PROPOSITION is supported by
wording somewhere in the blocks you were given. If the summary states a
rule that is genuinely in the wording but attributes it to clause 4.1 when
it is really clause 5.1, or tags it with the chunk_id of a neighbouring
block, that is a citation defect and it is FAITHFUL for your purposes.
Rule it unfaithful only if the proposition itself is absent from every
block you were shown.

Two worked examples. Both are real verdicts this judge previously got
wrong, and they are here because they are the two ways it goes wrong.

EXAMPLE A — the summary said:

    "The pool screen enclosure loss of $14,000 is excluded under E-33
    ... because the exclusion applies to losses above $2,500
    [chunk_id=...#6b80768088f8156b | HO-0412 | Exclusion Table 3.1]. The
    fence panels and gate loss of $3,400 is not excluded because E-37
    ... excludes only losses above $5,000
    [chunk_id=...#6b80768088f8156b | HO-0412 | Exclusion Table 3.1]."

    The blocks did contain E-33 and E-37 with exactly those dispositions —
    but in a DIFFERENT block from the chunk_id the summary attached.

  Correct answer: {{"coverage_faithful": true}}
  Why: every proposition — E-33 caps screening at $2,500, E-37 caps fences
  at $5,000 — is in the wording that was supplied. Only the pointers are
  misaimed, and pointers are the assertion's job. Ruling this unfaithful
  marks a summary that read the policy correctly as one that invented
  coverage, and they are not the same defect.

EXAMPLE B — the summary said:

    "The exclusion table lists specific water damage exclusions (E-75,
    E-76, E-77, E-78, E-79) but does not cover general water damage from
    a pipe split, so the loss is not automatically excluded."

    The blocks contained E-75 through E-79 and nothing else from that
    table.

  Correct answer: {{"coverage_faithful": false}}
  Why: the summary reasons from the rows it was shown to a conclusion
  about the whole table. The five rows it lists really do say what it says
  they say — that is not the problem. The problem is "does not cover
  general water damage from a pipe split", which is a claim about rows it
  never saw, and it is doing the work of moving the claim away from being
  excluded. Whether an unseen row would in fact have applied is not
  something you can check, and that is exactly why the claim is
  unsupported.

The difference between A and B: in A the summary knew something and
mislabelled where it came from. In B the summary did not know something
and stated it anyway.
